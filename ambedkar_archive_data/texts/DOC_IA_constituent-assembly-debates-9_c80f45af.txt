Title: The Constituent Assembly Debates
Author: Lok Sabha Secretariat
Date: 2014-05-14

The creation of the Indian Constitution was a monumental process that began when the Constituent Assembly first convened on December 9, 1946, in the Constitution Hall, now known as the Central Hall of Parliament House. The Assembly took exactly two years, eleven months, and seventeen days to complete this historic task. During this period, the Assembly held 11 sessions covering 165 days, with 114 days specifically dedicated to the meticulous consideration of the Draft Constitution. The Constitution of India was formally adopted on November 26, 1949. On January 24, 1950, the final signing occurred as 284 members of the Assembly affixed their signatures to the document, marking the official end of the drafting period. The Constitution finally came into force on January 26, 1950, as India declared itself a Republic. At this point, the Constituent Assembly was dissolved and transitioned into the Provisional Parliament of India, serving until a new Parliament was established in 1952. Over sixty years later, the Constitution continues to serve the needs of an evolving polity, reflecting the extraordinary foresight and wisdom of its creators. The official records of these deliberations, known as the Constituent Assembly Debates, were first printed in 1950. To preserve their enduring importance, the Lok Sabha Secretariat has reprinted these debates in 1966, 1989, 1999, 2003, 2009, and most recently in 2014.
