Title: Annihilation Of Caste
Author: Dr. B.R. Ambedkar
Date: 1950-01-26

Annihilation of Caste is a seminal essay by Indian social reformer and activist B. R. Ambedkar, first published in 1936. It critiques the Hindu caste system and argues for its complete eradication.  Historical Context - Written in response to a 1934 invitation from the Jat-Pat Todak Mandal, a reformist Hindu organization, to discuss caste abolition. - Ambedkar declined the invitation, stating that the organization’s members were themselves entrenched in caste interests, and instead published the essay independently Core Arguments Caste as a Tool of Oppression: Ambedkar describes caste as a “social, political, and economic institution” designed to maintain the dominance of the Brahminical elite. - Religion and Law: He contends that Hindu religious texts and legal codes legitimize caste hierarchy, making reform within the system impossible. - Need for Radical Change: Rather than incremental reforms, Ambedkar calls for the complete dismantling of caste and the adoption of a secular, egalitarian social order. Impact and Legacy - Influenced the drafting of the Indian Constitution, where Ambedkar served as chief architect, embedding principles of equality and non‑discrimination. - Continues to be a foundational text for Dalit movements, anti‑caste activism, and scholarly debates on social justice in India.
